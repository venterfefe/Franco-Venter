Bible Notes: Malachi 1:1–5

A reflection on spiritual drift, God's love, and the danger of becoming indifferent.

October 27, 2023

The book of Malachi is often overlooked. It’s not the most popular book in the Bible, nor is it the longest. Yet it carries a direct and weighty message from God to His people.

To understand Malachi, we need to understand the context. After years in exile, the Jewish people were allowed to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple. Leaders like Ezra and Nehemiah helped restore structure, law, and order among the people.

But despite this, something deeper was wrong. The people had become spiritually flat. Their hearts were no longer engaged.

A People Who Question God

In Malachi 1:2, God says:

“I have loved you,” says the Lord.

But the people respond:

“How have you loved us?”

This response reveals something serious. The people have lost their ability to be moved by God’s love. What should have produced humility and joy instead produces skepticism.

We see this pattern repeated. God speaks, and the people question. There is a growing distance between God’s word and their hearts.

The Danger of Spiritual Drift

Malachi exposes a deeper issue — not just outward disobedience, but inward indifference. The people still go through the motions, but their hearts are no longer aligned with God.

Jesus later describes this same condition:

“These people honor me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me.”

This is not just an Old Testament problem. It is a present danger. It is possible to appear faithful externally while drifting internally.

A Message for Us

It is easy to read Malachi and assume it applies only to others. But the warning is personal. The same tendencies — spiritual fatigue, indifference, quiet rebellion — exist in us as well.

As Christians, our responsibility is not only personal but communal. We are called to encourage one another and take responsibility for the spiritual health of God’s people.

Malachi reminds us that God’s people had grown tired of loving and serving Him. That is a sobering thought.

Closing Reflection

Malachi speaks with urgency because he carries the burden of God for His people. That same burden should shape how we live and how we serve others.

The question is not simply whether we believe the right things, but whether our hearts are still responsive to God’s word.

Source

These notes are based on a sermon preached at the Reformed Church of Sydney.
